Grief After Diagnosis: A Course for AuDHD Adults

Written lessons, short videos from Leah, and one live group session a week.

About the course

A five-module course for autistic, ADHD and AuDHD adults who have been diagnosed later in life and feel lost about what comes next. It is built from Leah's lived experience, with research woven through, and runs as written lessons plus one live group session a week.

Who it's for: autistic, ADHD and AuDHD adults in the months or years after a late diagnosis. It's written through an AuDHD lens, with notes wherever experiences tend to differ. It's especially for anyone feeling relief and grief at the same time and unsure where to turn.

What it is: education, reflection and peer connection, guided by a Registered Nurse and Master of Counselling student who has been through it.

What it isn't: therapy, counselling, diagnosis or medical advice. Anyone who needs more support is pointed to their GP and the services in the resources section.

Format:

  • Five modules, one released each week
  • 16 short lessons in total (3 to 4 per module, about 5 to 10 minutes each)
  • A short video from Leah in every lesson, with the written text underneath for people who prefer reading
  • One exercise and reflection prompts per module
  • One 60-minute live group session each week

The spine: every module follows the same four steps, so participants always know where they are.

  1. Recognise what is coming up
  2. Acknowledge it without judging it
  3. Understand how your AuDHD brain is part of the story
  4. Move forward with one small, doable step

The five modules and 16 lessons

  1. Module 1 · Week 1

    Naming it

    Relief and grief can arrive at the same time, often tangled up with shame and doubt. This module gives those feelings names, so they stop feeling like proof that something is wrong with you.

    • Lesson 1.1: Relief and grief can both be true (free preview)
    • Lesson 1.2: Shame, guilt and imposter syndrome
    • Lesson 1.3: Why it can feel so loud

  2. Module 2 · Week 2

    Recognise the losses

    Naming specific losses makes grief easier to hold than a vague heaviness. This module helps you put words to what you are grieving.

    • Lesson 2.1: Naming what you lost
    • Lesson 2.2: Grieving the masked years
    • Lesson 2.3: Why AuDHD losses can stack up

  3. Module 3 · Week 3

    Acknowledge the anger and the what ifs

    Anger is a normal part of grief. This module gives you permission to feel it, without letting it run the show.

    • Lesson 3.1: Where the anger points
    • Lesson 3.2: Envy and the "what if" loop
    • Lesson 3.3: Letting anger move through

  4. Module 4 · Week 4

    Understand who you are now

    A diagnosis can make you question which parts of you are really you. This module helps you separate your brain from the mask and the shame, and start unmasking at a pace that feels safe.

    • Lesson 4.1: Brain, mask and shame
    • Lesson 4.2: Sensory overload or anxiety?
    • Lesson 4.3: Unmasking safely
    • Lesson 4.4: Authenticity and self-compassion

  5. Module 5 · Week 5

    Move forward

    Grief doesn't finish on a schedule, and moving forward doesn't mean leaving it behind. This module is about carrying it more gently and building a life that fits your brain.

    • Lesson 5.1: Grief comes in waves
    • Lesson 5.2: Finding your people
    • Lesson 5.3: Your next chapter

Weekly live session

One 60-minute session per module, run the same way each week so it feels predictable and safe.

About Leah

Leah Veenhuizen is a Registered Nurse with over a decade of experience across acute, community, rural and public health settings, a certified Mental Health First Aid facilitator, and a Master of Counselling student. She is AuDHD and was diagnosed later in life, so this course comes from lived experience as well as study.

Leah runs this course as an educator and peer, not as a therapist or counsellor.

Common questions

Do I need a formal diagnosis?

No. Everyone is welcome, whether you have a formal diagnosis, are self-identified, or are still working it out.

Is this therapy?

No. This course offers education and peer connection from lived experience. It is not therapy, counselling, diagnosis or medical advice. If you need more support, please speak with your GP.

Where are the live sessions held?

Online. You will be emailed the link before the first session. Cameras, mics and sharing are always optional, and you are welcome to take part by chat only.

What if I miss a live session?

That's okay. The lessons, exercises and reflection prompts are there whenever you need them, and you can join the next session when you're ready.

What if the course brings up a lot for me?

Take breaks whenever you need to. Crisis and support lines are one click away on every page, through the "Need support now?" button.
