Lesson 1.2: Shame, guilt and imposter syndrome

For many people, grief isn't the first feeling to arrive. Shame, guilt and doubt often get there first.

Shame is the voice that says "you should have coped better." After decades of being told you were careless, rude or too much, that voice can keep running even after a diagnosis explains it. In an Australian study of newly diagnosed autistic adults, one participant described grief "because of how badly I treated myself for so many years" (Edwards et al., 2025 (opens in a new tab)).

Guilt can show up as "other people have it worse" or "I shouldn't be sad, I finally have answers." You're allowed to feel relieved and sad at once.

Imposter syndrome about your own diagnosis is extremely common: "Am I really autistic? Am I just making excuses? What if they got it wrong?" This loop can keep going long after the assessment. It doesn't mean your diagnosis is wrong. It often means you've spent years doubting your own experience.

These feelings tend to ease as understanding grows. They don't need to be fought, just noticed and named.
