Masking wasn't fake or weak. Research shows autistic adults mask for real reasons, like fitting in and avoiding being bullied or treated badly (Cage & Troxell-Whitman, 2019 (opens in a new tab)). That means unmasking should happen at a pace and in places that feel safe.

  • Start small and safe: at home, alone, or with one trusted person
  • Pick one thing: stim freely, skip forced eye contact, wear the comfy clothes, say no
  • Choose your spaces: it's okay to keep some masks at work while you drop others with friends
  • Expect wobbles: some people feel more vulnerable or exposed at first, and that's normal
  • Notice the relief: pay attention to what feels lighter when you stop performing

If you're autistic: your mask may be mostly social, such as scripted chats, forced eye contact and hiding stims.

If you're ADHD: it may be about looking organised, on time and in control.

If you're both: you may have been wearing two masks at once.
