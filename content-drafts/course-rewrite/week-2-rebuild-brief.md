## Proposed worksheet

**Title:** The Unmasking Map

Purpose: Help participants identify masking patterns, name what they cost, and begin the process of noticing rather than dismantling (not a "stop masking" exercise, as sudden unmasking in unsafe contexts carries risk).

Steps:
1. Name one masking behaviour you recognise in yourself
2. When did this pattern start? What situation required it?
3. What does this cost you? (Energy, identity, relationship authenticity, etc.)
4. Is this mask still load-bearing in your current life? Or is it a habit from an earlier context?
5. What would "noticing this" rather than "performing this" look like?

Note: This worksheet must not encourage participants to unmask in unsafe environments. Add a note: "Unmasking is not always safe or appropriate in every context. This exercise is about noticing, not about immediate change."

## Proposed facilitator session topic

Week 2 live catch-up: "What does your mask look like, and what has it cost?"

Structure:
- 5-minute welcome and Week 1 check-in
- 10-minute teaching recap of masking concepts
- 15-minute small group or paired reflection: share one masking pattern and one cost
- 10-minute group discussion: what did appearing fine require of you?
- 10-minute facilitator input: self-blame versus self-awareness
- 10-minute close: week 3 preview, scope reminder, support signposting

Note: The facilitator must monitor for distress. The unmasking topic can surface grief, shame and anger. Follow the scope boundary guidance in the Facilitator Guide.

## Evidence areas needed for Week 2

- Research on social camouflaging in ADHD (particularly late-diagnosed adults)
- Research on camouflaging in autism (relevant for AuDHD participants)
- Research on perfectionism in ADHD: link to rejection sensitivity and executive function
- Shame and internalised stigma in late-diagnosed adults
- Cognitive load of masking as documented in the literature
- Identity and self-concept after late diagnosis, particularly the unmasking phase

Do not use RSD (rejection-sensitive dysphoria) as a term. Describe the experience without the proprietary framing.

Do not present unmasking as straightforwardly positive or as the goal. The research is clear that unmasking in unsafe contexts carries risk.
