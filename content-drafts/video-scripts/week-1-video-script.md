## Script

---

Welcome to Week 1.

The title of this week is The Late Diagnosis Disruption, and I chose the word disruption deliberately.

Because that is what it is. A late ADHD diagnosis is not simply receiving a piece of paper that says something new. It is information arriving inside a life that was already built, built around explanations that were wrong, or incomplete, or missing entirely.

And when that information arrives, things start to shift. Sometimes quietly. Sometimes all at once. Often in waves that keep coming back.

That is what this week is about.

---

The first thing I want to name is the life review.

After a late ADHD diagnosis, many adults start looking back. Not in a deliberate, organised way. It often just starts happening.

You find yourself thinking about school. About jobs you lost. About relationships that fell apart. About all the times you were called lazy, or disorganised, or too sensitive, or too much, or not enough.

And the new information, the ADHD understanding, starts getting laid over all of it.

Some things suddenly make more sense. Some things hurt more now that you can see them clearly. Some things you are not yet sure what to do with.

This is a completely normal part of late diagnosis. It has been named in the research. It is not you being dramatic or self-pitying or dwelling. It is what happens when accurate information arrives late.

---

I want to say something important here, and I will say it more than once across this course.

Understanding your ADHD is not an excuse for everything that has happened. But it is a better explanation than the ones you were probably given.

"You are not trying hard enough" was not an accurate explanation.
"You are too sensitive" was not an accurate explanation.
"You are lazy" was not an accurate explanation.
"You just need to be more organised" was not an accurate explanation.

A more accurate explanation involves executive functioning, which we will get into in detail in Week 3. The short version is this: ADHD affects your ability to initiate tasks, manage time, regulate emotions, hold information in working memory, plan, follow through and recover from difficulty. Not because you are not trying. Because the neurological processes involved in those things work differently in ADHD brains.

That is not a character flaw. It is a neurological difference that has been misread, for a very long time, as a character flaw.

---

The emotional response to late diagnosis is different for everyone.

Some people feel immediate relief. A framework that finally fits. Permission to stop blaming themselves for things that were never a choice.

Some people feel grief. Grief for what might have been different. For the years spent fighting against their own brain with the wrong tools. For the diagnosis that did not come in time to change something important.

Some people feel anger. At clinicians who missed it. At schools that punished it. At family members who had a name for it that was wrong.

Some people feel nothing yet. Numbness. A kind of "I will wait and see if this is real."

All of these are valid responses to receiving important information late.

I am not going to tell you how to feel about your diagnosis. What I want to offer is a framework for making sense of what is already coming up, and a space to do that without being rushed toward a particular conclusion.

---

This week has four sections in the written content. They follow the same structure every week will use.

Recognise: What are you noticing differently now?
Acknowledge: What is the emotional response, and what does it deserve?
Understand: The concepts and frameworks that explain what you are experiencing.
Move Forward: One small, practical thing to try.

Each section has a workbook prompt. You do not have to complete all of them. Work with the ones that feel live for you.

---

This week's milestone is this: identify three things from your past that look different now that you have your ADHD understanding.

Not three things you have resolved. Not three things you are proud of. Just three things that you are now seeing more clearly.

You can tick that milestone in the platform when it feels true. There is no deadline.

---

This week's Companion Guide is Grieving the Life You Thought You Had. If the grief side of this week is landing heavily, that guide goes deeper. It is available in your Resource Vault.

And if you have not already worked through The Late Diagnosis Life Review, that is also worth your time. It is a structured exercise designed to help you map the patterns in your life with your ADHD understanding. You can find it in the downloads section.

---

I will leave you with this.

You do not have to have this sorted by the end of the week. You do not have to have the right feelings, the right reframes or the right words for what happened. The aim of Week 1 is simply to start seeing things more clearly.

That is enough.

I will see you in Week 2, where we go into masking, and why appearing fine is one of the most exhausting and costly things many late-diagnosed ADHD adults have ever done.
