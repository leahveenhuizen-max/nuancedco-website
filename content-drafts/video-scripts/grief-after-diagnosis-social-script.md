## Script

Nobody tells you about the grief that comes after an ADHD diagnosis.

They tell you about relief. About finally having an answer. About the door that just opened.

And that is real. But so is the other thing.

The grief for the version of your life where someone figured it out earlier.
The grief for the school years where you were told you were not trying.
The grief for the relationship that ended because the miscommunication went on too long.
The grief for the career you left because you could not make the system work for how your brain works.
The grief for the version of yourself that spent years believing what they were told about you.

That is real grief. And it is a completely valid response to receiving important information twenty, thirty or forty years later than it should have arrived.

The thing I want to say is this: grief after late diagnosis is not the same as self-pity.

Grief asks, "what mattered here?" Self-pity says, "this was all my fault."

Those are different things.

Understanding the difference is part of what The Unmasked Life course is for.

It is four weeks of research-informed education for late-diagnosed ADHD adults who want to understand their brain, their past and their support needs without shame-based self-improvement.
