## Script

Masking is not lying. But it is exhausting.

Masking is the process of hiding, suppressing or compensating for neurodivergent traits to appear more neurotypical — or more capable, or more fine, or more whatever the situation seems to require.

In ADHD, it might look like:

Over-preparing obsessively so no one notices the last-minute chaos.
Laughing things off when you are actually overwhelmed.
Saying yes when you mean "I have no capacity for this."
Performing enthusiasm you do not feel because low-motivation ADHD is being read as rudeness.
Being the most reliable person in the room so no one looks too closely at what it is costing you.

Masking does not mean your ADHD is not real.
It means you became very good at making it invisible.

And for many late-diagnosed adults, the unmasking process — which starts after diagnosis — is one of the hardest things they have to navigate. Not because stopping masking is easy. But because the mask has been load-bearing for a very long time.

The Unmasked Life course goes into this in depth. Week 2 is specifically about masking, overcompensation and self-blame.
