# Award-Level Redesign Strategy

The strongest available direction for the Nuanced Co. website and The Unmasked Life course, based on the benchmark research, pattern analysis and audits in this folder.

---

## Core creative idea: "New language for an old map"

The brand already contains its big idea; it just has not committed to it. Week 1's Old Map / New Map metaphor, the constellation dots in the production candidate, and the flagship line "Late diagnosis is not a personality flaw. It is the absence of language you always deserved" are all the same thought:

**You have been navigating with a map that was labelled wrong. Nuanced Co. re-labels the map.**

This becomes the unifying creative device across everything:

- **The website** is the daylight side of the map: cream, warm, oriented, "here is the territory, here is where to start".
- **The course** is the night side: dark cocoa and plum, constellations, one landmark at a time, ending each week with a re-labelled fragment of the learner's own map (the Snapshot).
- **The PDFs** are field guides and charts: artefacts from the same cartography.
- **The language system** follows: markers, landmarks, routes (already used in "low-capacity route"), legends (already used in "Map Legend" on the Snapshot). This vocabulary exists in the work today; the strategy is to make it deliberate and visible rather than incidental.

This idea is specific to Leah's material (a life reviewed and re-labelled after late diagnosis), unowned in the ADHD education space (competitors own doodles, Venn diagrams, planners and toolboxes; nobody owns cartography), and it is inherently non-clinical and non-Canva.

## Website visual direction

- **Light, warm, editorial.** Cream field, cocoa text, plum and ochre as accent only. Large Cormorant Garamond headlines, Atkinson Hyperlegible body. More whitespace discipline than now: whitespace with content in view, not empty viewports.
- **The map motif drawn, not described:** a fine-line topographic/constellation illustration system used as section dividers, card backgrounds and the hero. Replace or evolve the watercolour blob mark into a tighter mark (overlapping forms redrawn as intersecting map contours keeps continuity).
- **Photography:** Leah, warmly lit, consistently treated (the existing arch-and-underline device is good and should be systematised). No stock imagery anywhere.
- **First viewport rule:** headline + one-sentence positioning + one CTA + Leah's presence within two screens. The logo becomes small and confident, not the hero.

## Course visual direction

- Adopt the Week 1 production candidate as the design system of record and elevate it: night-side palette, constellation wayfinding (the stepper dots become literal stars on a route), one crafted map illustration per week that gains a new label as the week completes.
- Scene text cut to Inflow density: one idea per screen beat, generous line height, breathing room.
- Leah audio on every scene where "A note from Leah" exists today; her voice is the warmth layer that typography cannot supply.
- The weekly artefact (Snapshot) gets the most design investment of anything in the course: it is what learners keep, print and screenshot. Make it beautiful enough to share.

## How website and course connect

Same typefaces, same motif, day/night inversion of the same palette. The sales page shows a real (sample) Snapshot and one real scene screenshot, so the buyer sees the night side from the daylight side. The transition moment (post-purchase welcome) explicitly crosses from day to night: "The public site showed you the territory. This is where we walk it."

## Sequence: what changes first

1. **Truth pass (before any design):** delete/redirect fossil pages, fix the counselling-practice meta description, fix the contact form 404, align every week title and milestone to the locked sequence, strip em dashes and repair comma collisions, remove fake dashboard state, gate or noindex paid content.
2. **Ladder pass:** one page per product rung with price and previews; Life Review gets an on-site page; Reader Edition and Companion Guides appear publicly; the two waitlists get distinct names.
3. **Homepage rebuild** on the new hero rule.
4. **Course template promotion:** candidate becomes week-1.html; weeks 2 to 4 rebuilt on it with migrated content.
5. **Presence pass:** Leah photo on the sales page, audio notes recorded, PDF interior previews shot.
6. Only then: motif illustration commission/build and progressive visual elevation.

## What should not change

- The voice. The best copy lines are the brand's most valuable IP after Leah herself.
- The locked course sequence and the four-stage weekly framework (Recognise, Acknowledge, Understand, Move Forward).
- The emotional-safety machinery: route choice, formats, Hit a Wall, support page structure, scope notes, "tick when it feels true for you".
- The type pairing and core palette.
- The education-only scope discipline: no drift, ever.
- The Snapshot artefact concept and its copy/print behaviour.

## What should be removed

- Pages: workbooks.html, course.html, enrol.html, portal.html, consult.html, course-mark.html, courses.html, podcast.html (fold), quiz.html (unless remapped), pricing.html (unless rebuilt as the full-ladder page).
- The duplicate About section; the duplicate Week 1 (once the candidate is promoted); week-1-experience-prototype.html from the deploy directory.
- Subscribe blocks and press-kit links inside the course portal.
- Emojis on links.html; the ™ (unless registration exists); the Pomodoro timer branding as "Sprint"/"Matrix" language.
- All placeholder strings from learner-reachable surfaces.

## What should be simplified

- Navigation to five items: Start Here (or Home), The Course, Guides and Resources, Work with Leah (individuals + workplaces), About. Support and legal in the footer.
- The homepage to one narrative: problem → who Leah is → the ladder's three rungs → one CTA.
- The two waitlists into clearly named, differently styled captures (Course waitlist vs Occasional updates).
- Course tools into one capacity model (route choice at entry; one toggle inside).

## What should be made more premium

- The Snapshot artefact (typographic keepsake design, beautiful print CSS).
- The paid PDFs: keepsake-grade covers and interiors from the map world, with visible previews before purchase.
- The dashboard: true state, calm labels ("Your map so far", not "Sprint Progress Tracker"), course-only footer.
- Leah's presence: professional-warm photography set, recorded audio, a signed note in every product.
- Microcopy at moments of return ("Welcome back. Nothing expired while you were away.").

## Where Leah's presence should appear

Homepage screen two (face, name, one-sentence credibility). Course sales page bio (photo, not placeholder). Every course week (audio note minimum). PDF opening pages (short signed note). FAQ scope answers (first person). Nowhere should the brand feel authorless; a solo-founder education brand's founder is the trust engine.

## Where evidence should appear

A public Evidence page (approach + key sources, APA 7). "What the research shows" asides inside course scenes with real citations. References section in every paid PDF. "Content reviewed [date]" on each course week. One or two quantified, sourced claims on the sales page. Never as decoration: every claim traceable.

## Where support boundaries should appear

Every product page (one warm sentence + link). Every heavy course scene (the candidate's pattern is already right). The dashboard (Hit a Wall stays). support.html linked from every course footer. The boundary language stays warm and first person, never legal-defensive in body copy (the legal pages carry the formal version).

## How to avoid generic ADHD-course design

No brain illustrations, no lightning bolts, no rainbow infinity clip-art, no "chaos to calm" before/after tropes, no productivity-bro dashboard aesthetics, no icon-grid feature sections without craft. The cartography world plus literary typography is the moat; commit to it.

## How to avoid over-clinical design

No white-coat blues, no checklist-and-symptom framing, no third-person case language. Keep first person, keep warmth in the palette, keep Leah's voice audible. The scope note formula stays "education, not therapy" in warm words, not disclaimers shouting from every corner (once per surface is enough).

## How to avoid Canva-workbook design

No template gradients, no sticker icons, no decorative fonts, no A4 pages of centred display type. Typeset the PDFs like books (grid, margins, running heads, page numbers). Show interiors publicly: transparency is itself the anti-Canva signal.

## Commercially clear without feeling salesy

The School of Life/NDI pattern: state price plainly on every product page, show the actual product (previews, sample scene, sample Snapshot), use specific nouns (four weeks, six scenes, three formats), one CTA per screen, and let scarcity be real or absent (founding beta seat count only if true). No countdowns, no crossed-out prices, no exclamation marks. Desire comes from seeing a beautiful, specific thing that obviously matches your situation.
