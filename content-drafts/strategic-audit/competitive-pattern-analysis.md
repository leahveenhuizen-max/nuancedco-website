# Competitive Pattern Analysis

What the successful brands in the benchmark set share, and where Nuanced Co. currently stands against each pattern. Companion to global-benchmark-research.md.

---

## Pattern 1: Positioning is one sentence, said the same way everywhere

Every strong brand in the set can be reduced to a sentence a stranger could repeat: "research-backed ADHD coaching" (Unconventional Organisation), "the ultimate autism resource" (Embrace Autism), "an emotional education" (School of Life), "a planner built for ADHD brains" (Future ADHD).

**Nuanced Co. today:** the positioning exists ("research-informed ADHD education for late-diagnosed adults") but the site does not say it. The homepage hero says nothing at all above the fold. The nav says "Neurodivergent Consultancy". The pricing page sells 1:1 sessions. The links page sells a quiz. A visitor cannot repeat the sentence because the site never commits to it. **Underperforming.**

## Pattern 2: The first screen answers "what is this and is it for me"

Inflow, Future ADHD, NDI and Ness Labs all put audience plus promise plus action in the first viewport. Nobody spends the first screen on their own logo.

**Nuanced Co. today:** the entire first desktop viewport is the logo mark, wordmark and location. The actual headline ("You don't need more discipline. You need better structure.") is hidden below the fold behind a scroll animation. This is the single most expensive weakness on the site. **Severely underperforming.**

## Pattern 3: A legible product ladder with one entry point

Winners have an obvious "start here": NDI's free article to workbook, Ness Labs' newsletter to membership, Gottman's book to workshop. Each rung names its price and audience.

**Nuanced Co. today:** the intended ladder (Life Review PDF at $19, Companion Guides, Reader Edition, full course) is coherent on paper but the site presents fragments of three different eras at once: a pricing page selling a discontinued 7-module course, an enrol/portal/course.html funnel from an old build, a Stan link, a waitlist, and session packages. The Reader Edition and Companion Guides appear nowhere on the public site. **Severely underperforming, and this is mostly a deletion job, not a build job.**

## Pattern 4: Course structure is promised specifically and delivered visibly

Gottman quantifies ("94% report positive results"); Inflow shows module lists; Future ADHD counts pages. Buyers see exactly what they get.

**Nuanced Co. today:** the-unmasked-life.html actually does this reasonably well (four weeks, milestones, what's included). But the week descriptions on that page describe the old course order (grief milestone under the Masking title, masking milestone under the Support Needs title), so the promise contradicts the locked sequence. **Mixed; broken by content drift.**

## Pattern 5: Community is either real or absent, never implied

Strong brands only show community when it exists (How to ADHD's Brains; Ness Labs' member count). Nobody fakes it.

**Nuanced Co. today:** "weekly catch-up" cards carry "[to be confirmed] · Join link: [to be added]" placeholders, and the community page is a waitlist. Acceptable pre-launch, but placeholders must never ship to paying learners. **At risk.**

## Pattern 6: Pricing is stated, singular and unembarrassed

Future ADHD and NDI put prices on product pages. School of Life prices everything like a bookshop. Nobody hides price behind mailto links.

**Nuanced Co. today:** the course price appears once, on the homepage ("from $197 AUD"), and never on the course sales page. Session pricing lives on an orphaned pricing page whose CTAs are mailto links. The Life Review is $19 on the links page but unpriced elsewhere. **Underperforming.**

## Pattern 7: Visual identity has one ownable device

Mini ADHD Coach has the doodle character. NDI has the Venn diagram. School of Life has its typographic system. Future ADHD has the planner spreads. One device, repeated until it is theirs.

**Nuanced Co. today:** the ingredients exist (Cormorant Garamond and Atkinson Hyperlegible pairing, the plum/cream/dark-cocoa palette, the watercolour blob mark, and, in the course, the Old Map / New Map constellation motif). But no single device is developed and repeated. The blob mark is soft and forgettable; the constellation dots in the Week 1 candidate are the strongest candidate for an ownable device and they are nearly invisible. **Underperforming, with a clear path.**

## Pattern 8: Founder story is compressed into credibility, not biography

Winners state the founder in one breath: "AuDHD clinical psychologist", "neuroscientist at King's College", "diagnosed at 29". Lived experience and authority in the same sentence, then they move on.

**Nuanced Co. today:** Leah's material is genuinely strong (Registered Nurse, AHPRA; mental health educator; AuDHD; late-diagnosed; Master of Counselling in progress) but the about page says it twice in two half-built sections ("Hey! I'm Leah…" then "Hi, I'm Leah." with the same photo repeated), the course sales page bio has an empty grey placeholder circle instead of a photo, and the homepage barely mentions her. **Underperforming despite strong raw material.**

## Pattern 9: Evidence is specific or silent

Embrace Autism footnotes everything. Gottman counts couples and years. Inflow ran an RCT. The pattern: either show the receipt or do not make the claim.

**Nuanced Co. today:** "research-informed" and "evidence-informed" appear throughout, but no public page shows a single citation, reference list or "reviewed against literature" note. The Evidence Base document is listed in the vault as a placeholder. For a Registered Nurse positioning on research-informed education, this is the cheapest unclaimed win on the board. **Underperforming.**

## Pattern 10: Tone: warm, adult, unhurried, shame-aware

The best copy in this space (NDI, School of Life, Finch, Inflow) shares four properties: it never moralises, it anticipates low capacity, it treats the reader as intelligent, and it does not perform enthusiasm.

**Nuanced Co. today: this is the strongest thing Nuanced Co. has.** Lines like "Late diagnosis is not a personality flaw. It is the absence of language you always deserved" and the candidate's "You do not need to finish this today. There is no deadline" are genuinely at benchmark level. The voice is the asset. The problem is everything around the voice: typos (comma-collision artefacts like "your actual life,the past" where dashes were stripped), em dashes surviving in the course candidate against brand rules, and off-register labels like "Sprint Progress Tracker" and "Milestone Matrix" in the dashboard. **At benchmark in draft, below benchmark in QA.**

## Pattern 11: Emotional safety is a designed system, not a disclaimer

Finch designs for the returning user. Inflow designs for the drop-off. The support boundary appears at the moment of need, not only in the footer.

**Nuanced Co. today:** genuinely good instincts: the "Hit a wall?" card, scope notes inside heavy scenes, the support page with Australian crisis lines, "listening-only participation is welcome". This is close to benchmark. The support page still lists the old week titles, which breaks trust at the exact moment it matters most. **Near benchmark; one consistency fix away.**

## Pattern 12: Accessibility is a feature, marketed as such

How to ADHD sells its book partly on its ADHD-friendly layout. Inflow's pacing is the product. Format choice is presented as respect, not accommodation.

**Nuanced Co. today:** three-format lessons, low-capacity route, reduce-motion and focus toggles, Atkinson Hyperlegible. Strong. But the public site never mentions any of it as a selling point beyond one small card, and week-1.html (the non-candidate version) is a wall of accordions that contradicts the promise. **Good machinery, unsold.**

## Pattern 13: Mobile is the primary reading context

Every consumer brand in the set assumes phone-first evening reading.

**Nuanced Co. today:** the public site holds up at 390px (no horizontal overflow, readable type). The Week 1 candidate mostly works but has a floating "My Snapshot" chip colliding with content at the bottom of the viewport, and the header wraps untidily. **Adequate, needs polish.**

## Pattern 14: Desire is created through specificity, not hype

The pattern across every trusted brand: concrete nouns (270 pages, six scenes, four weeks, 45 minutes), previews of the actual artefact, and restraint everywhere else. Urgency mechanics appear only in the mass-market tier (Mindvalley) and cost trust.

**Nuanced Co. today:** the restraint is there; the specificity and the previews are not. No interior spreads of the Life Review PDF, no sample scene of the course, no screenshot of the Snapshot tool. The most desirable things Nuanced Co. has built are invisible at the point of sale. **Underperforming.**

---

## Summary: where Nuanced Co. underperforms most

Ranked by commercial damage:

1. **Product-ladder incoherence** (three eras of offers live simultaneously; Reader Edition and Companion Guides absent; pricing contradictory).
2. **First-screen failure** on the homepage (logo-only viewport).
3. **Content drift between locked sequence and shipped pages** (weeks 2 to 4 bodies, sales-page milestones, support-page titles).
4. **Invisible founder credibility** (placeholder avatar on the sales page; duplicated about page).
5. **Unsubstantiated evidence claims** (no visible references anywhere public).
6. **No product previews** (PDFs and course sold sight-unseen).
7. **QA debris undermining a premium voice** (typos, placeholders, dead links, legacy pages).

And where it already matches the winners: voice, emotional-safety instincts, format flexibility, scope discipline (no diagnosis/therapy drift found anywhere), and Australian crisis-support signposting.
