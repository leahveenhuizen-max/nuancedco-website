# Ruthless Quality Scorecard

Scored July 2026 against the global benchmark set (see global-benchmark-research.md), not against "fine for a solo founder". A 9 or 10 means it would hold its own next to Neurodivergent Insights, The School of Life or Inflow.

---

## Brand clarity: 4/10

- **Reason:** the positioning sentence exists in strategy but the site never says it; nav claims "consultancy", a legacy meta tag claims "counselling practice", and the course claims education. Three self-descriptions compete.
- **9/10 looks like:** one sentence ("research-informed ADHD education for late-diagnosed adults, from a registered nurse who is one") repeated verbatim on homepage, About, course page and bios.
- **Highest-impact fix:** commit the sentence to the homepage hero and delete every page that contradicts it.

## Homepage impact: 2/10

- **Reason:** the first viewport is a logo. The genuinely strong headline is hidden below the fold behind a scroll animation, and full-page rendering leaves large dead zones. Four CTAs across two indistinguishable waitlists.
- **9/10 looks like:** headline, positioning line, single CTA and Leah's presence within two screens; the "Late diagnosis is not a personality flaw" line doing hero duty somewhere prominent.
- **Highest-impact fix:** rebuild the hero. Nothing else on the site returns more per hour of work.

## Visual distinctiveness: 4/10

- **Reason:** good ingredients (type pairing, palette, arch photo treatment, constellation hints) with no committed device. The blob mark is forgettable; most sections are generic card grids any template could produce.
- **9/10 looks like:** the map/constellation world drawn and repeated until recognisable from a cropped screenshot, the way NDI's Venn diagrams are.
- **Highest-impact fix:** commission/build the map illustration system and apply it to hero, dividers and PDF covers.

## Course experience: 4/10

- **Reason:** the Week 1 candidate is genuinely strong architecture (scenes, routes, Snapshot artefact) at 70 percent polish, but the shipped course is week-1.html's text wall plus three weeks of the wrong content under new titles, with no voice, no imagery and placeholder promises.
- **9/10 looks like:** all four weeks on the candidate template, locked-sequence content, Leah audio in every week, one crafted visual per week, artefact continuity between weeks.
- **Highest-impact fix:** execute the content migration and promote the candidate to template.

## Mobile experience: 6/10

- **Reason:** no horizontal overflow, readable type, sensible stacking across public and course pages. Deductions for the Snapshot chip overlapping content at 390px, header wrapping, and dashboard density.
- **9/10 looks like:** flawless phone-in-bed walkthrough of the full course; floating elements never touch content.
- **Highest-impact fix:** candidate mobile polish pass (chip, header, spacing).

## Product ladder clarity: 2/10

- **Reason:** the Reader Edition and Companion Guides appear nowhere public; a discontinued 7-module course is still advertised on an orphaned pricing page; the course price appears only on the homepage; the Life Review is an unexplained external link; free/paid boundaries are inverted.
- **9/10 looks like:** one page per rung with price, audience, previews and the next step; a stranger can recite the ladder after five minutes.
- **Highest-impact fix:** delete fossil offers, then publish the ladder (even with "coming" states for unbuilt rungs).

## Paid-product desirability: 3/10

- **Reason:** nothing paid is shown. No PDF interiors, no course scene screenshots, no sample Snapshot, no price on the sales page, placeholder bio photo. Restraint without specificity produces indifference, not desire.
- **9/10 looks like:** Future ADHD-style previews: three interior spreads per PDF, a live sample scene, a beautiful example Snapshot, plain prices.
- **Highest-impact fix:** Life Review product page with interior previews.

## Trust and credibility: 4/10

- **Reason:** strong legal pages, real credentials and honest scope copy fight against 404s (contact success, journal.html), fake dashboard progress, placeholder dates, dead links and fossil pages. Trust is a systems property and the system leaks.
- **9/10 looks like:** the trust standard test passing monthly: zero 404s, zero placeholders, true state everywhere.
- **Highest-impact fix:** the P0 truth pass (forms, links, placeholders, fossils).

## Evidence positioning: 2/10

- **Reason:** "research-informed" and "evidence-informed" recur with zero visible citations anywhere public; the Evidence Base vault item is a placeholder. For an RN-led brand this is the widest gap between claim and display.
- **9/10 looks like:** Embrace Autism-grade referencing adapted to Leah's voice: public evidence page, per-week references, reviewed dates.
- **Highest-impact fix:** publish the Evidence page and add references to Week 1.

## Emotional safety: 8/10

- **Reason:** the best dimension. Route choice, three formats, Hit a Wall, "no deadline, ever", scope notes at point of need, excellent Australian crisis signposting, listening-only participation. Deductions: support.html shows old week titles (a failure at the worst possible moment) and week-1.html's density contradicts the low-capacity promise.
- **9/10 looks like:** the same system with consistent titles and genuinely shortened low-capacity content.
- **Highest-impact fix:** correct support.html titles today.

## Accessibility: 7/10

- **Reason:** Atkinson Hyperlegible, reduce-motion and focus toggles, format choice, autosave: well ahead of the category. Deductions: contrast of low-opacity text on dark backgrounds in the course, accordion state announcement, unlabelled mobile stepper dots, and no visible skip links.
- **9/10 looks like:** WCAG 2.2 AA verified plus the ADHD-specific layer marketed as a feature.
- **Highest-impact fix:** contrast and aria pass on the course template before it multiplies fourfold.

## Conversion clarity: 3/10

- **Reason:** competing CTAs, two look-alike waitlists, price absent from the sales page, mailto CTAs on the (orphaned) pricing page, quiz funnel pointing at retired offers, contact form 404ing on success.
- **9/10 looks like:** one primary CTA per screen, each rung's page closing to exactly one action.
- **Highest-impact fix:** fix the contact form, then the CTA hierarchy on index and the sales page.

## Founder presence: 3/10

- **Reason:** Leah is the brand's rarest asset (RN + educator + AuDHD + late-diagnosed) and she is a placeholder circle on the money page, a duplicated half-page on About, an unrecorded audio label throughout the course, and nearly absent from the homepage.
- **9/10 looks like:** face or voice within two screens of any entry point; audio in every course week.
- **Highest-impact fix:** photo on the sales page and one recorded welcome note; hours, not weeks.

## Commercial readiness: 2/10

- **Reason:** the site cannot currently take money for the flagship (waitlist only, correct for now) while still advertising a discontinued course, contradictory prices, and a paid product accessible free at public URLs. Beta terms, seat counts and the founding offer exist nowhere public.
- **9/10 looks like:** ladder published, paid content protected, beta offer stated, forms working, refund/terms aligned to actual products.
- **Highest-impact fix:** P0.3 (protect course) plus P0.5 (delete fossil offers).

## Consistency: 2/10

- **Reason:** three navigation systems, we/I voice drift, two course visual generations, two Week 1s, ™ sometimes, week titles differing across four surfaces, dash policy violated in both directions.
- **9/10 looks like:** any two screens obviously the same brand; automated copy checks passing.
- **Highest-impact fix:** the deletions plus one nav/footer unification.

## Premium feel: 3/10

- **Reason:** premium is the absence of abrasion. Every typo, placeholder, dead link, fake number and fossil page is abrasion; the site currently has dozens. The candidate shows the ceiling is high; the floor is what visitors mostly touch.
- **9/10 looks like:** School of Life calm: few things, all finished.
- **Highest-impact fix:** finish or hide everything unfinished (the truth pass again; it keeps being the answer).

## Global competitiveness: 3/10

- **Reason:** against the benchmark set today, Nuanced Co. loses on polish, previews, evidence display and coherence, while holding two genuinely rare cards: the emotional-safety machinery and an Australian RN founder with lived experience in a market of US/UK offers. Nobody in the set owns "late diagnosis" as completely as this brand could.
- **9/10 looks like:** the niche's definitive late-diagnosis education brand, with the course experience as proof.
- **Highest-impact fix:** everything in P0/P1; the strategy is sound, the execution debt is the gap.

---

**Summary:** average 3.6, with a bimodal shape: the thinking (voice, safety design, scope discipline, course architecture) scores 7 to 8; the shipped surface (coherence, truth, previews, presence) scores 2 to 3. The gap is execution debt, not strategy debt. That is the good news: deletion, alignment and finishing are cheaper than reinvention.
