Objective test: can a first-time visitor answer each question below from the live site within two minutes? Assessed against the locked product model (The Unmasked Life full course; Reader Edition; Companion Guides including Grieving the Life You Thought You Had; The Late Diagnosis Life Review via Stan).

**Score: 4 clear answers out of 17.** The brand is not confusing because the thinking is confused; it is confusing because the site still exhibits every previous version of the thinking.

---

## The confusing, duplicated and weak pathways, enumerated

1. **Two waitlists, one appearance.** "Join the waitlist" (course) and "Join updates" / "Join the waitlist" (community) are visually identical CTAs. A visitor cannot tell which list they joined.
2. **Three "how I/we work" pages.** services.html, how-i-work.html, who-i-work-with.html say overlapping things in two different grammatical persons.
3. **Two course sales surfaces plus two funnels.** the-unmasked-life.html (current) and course.html/enrol.html/portal.html (fossil funnel still reachable via About's "Enrol Now").
4. **Two pricing realities.** Homepage: course from $197. Pricing page: sessions $220/$240/$590 and a discontinued course. Course page: no price.
5. **Two Week 1s** (plus a prototype) with the dashboard pointing at the older one.
6. **Two names sharing "Life Review".** The paid PDF (The Late Diagnosis Life Review) and the in-course exercise (The Life Review Map) will be conflated by buyers.
7. **Two brand voices.** "We" on current pages, "I" on legacy pages; two different navs and footers.
8. **Free/paid boundary inverted.** The paid course is publicly open; meanwhile "free" is used to describe substantive guides that policy says should not be free, while the actually-free crisis directory (support.html) is buried in the course area rather than offered publicly as the trust-builder it is.

## What the ladder should look like on-site (target state)

One page or section per rung, each naming price, audience and the next rung up:

1. **Free:** support and service-navigation resources (crisis lines, GP preparation, diagnosis pathway navigation). Publicly offered, generous, on-brand, policy-compliant.
2. **$19:** The Late Diagnosis Life Review: needs its own on-site page (overview, interior preview, who it is for, Stan buy button) instead of a bare external link.
3. **Companion Guides** (standalone paid PDFs; also included in the course): one index page; Grieving the Life You Thought You Had first.
4. **Reader Edition** (paid PDF of core course teaching): one page, positioned as "the course as a book, without the guided experience".
5. **The Unmasked Life full course** ($197+, founding beta first): the flagship page, carrying price, beta terms, and explicit "includes all Companion Guides and the Reader Edition content" ladder logic.
6. **Work with Leah / Workplaces:** consultancy kept clearly separate from the education ladder.

Every rung must answer: what it is, who it is for, what it costs, what the next step is. Nothing else on the site should offer a path that is not one of these rungs.
