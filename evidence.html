Our evidence approach

What research-informed
actually means here.

Nuanced Co. makes a specific claim: research-informed ADHD education for late-diagnosed adults. This page explains what that claim covers, what it does not, and how the content is built.

The claim

Research-informed, in plain terms.

Every teaching claim in Nuanced Co. materials is built from published research, recognised clinical guidance or established psychoeducational frameworks, translated into plain language for adults who were diagnosed late. Where the evidence is mixed or still emerging, the materials say so rather than presenting a tidy answer. Where something is Leah's professional observation or lived experience rather than research, it is framed that way.

What it does not mean

The honest boundaries.

  • It does not mean the content is clinical care, or that reading it produces clinical outcomes. Nuanced Co. provides education and structured reflection only: not therapy, counselling, diagnosis, treatment, medication advice or crisis support.
  • It does not mean every sentence carries a citation. Educational writing needs to breathe. The claims that carry weight are the ones checked against the literature.
  • It does not mean the research is settled. ADHD research, particularly on late diagnosis, masking and adult experience, is active and evolving. Materials carry review dates so you can see when they were last checked.

Australian guidance first

Built for the Australian context.

Nuanced Co. content prioritises Australian guidance and systems where they exist: the Australian evidence-based clinical practice guideline for ADHD developed by AADPA, Australian professional bodies, and public health navigation through services like Healthdirect. International research is used where it is the best available evidence, but pathways, terminology and service information are written for people navigating Australian systems.

Evidence areas

Where the course content draws from.

Our courses and guides draw on published research and recognised frameworks in areas including:

  • Adult ADHD presentation and the experience of late diagnosis
  • Masking and camouflaging in ADHD and autistic adults
  • Executive function and environmental support
  • Self-concept, self-blame and identity after diagnosis
  • Psychoeducation as a recognised, non-clinical form of support

Who builds it

Professional background plus lived experience.

Nuanced Co. content is created by Leah Veenhuizen, a neurodivergent consultant, registered nurse and mental health educator, currently completing postgraduate counselling studies. Leah is late-diagnosed AuDHD. The professional background shapes how evidence is read and translated; the lived experience shapes what questions get asked of it. Neither is presented as a substitute for the other.
