Speed-read — Week 1

This week is about the first wave of recognition after a late ADHD diagnosis.

Many adults begin looking back and noticing patterns they had previously labelled as laziness, inconsistency, messiness, sensitivity, failure or not trying hard enough.

Recognise: Notice what you are now seeing differently.
Acknowledge: Make space for the emotional response, whether that is relief, grief, anger, hope, confusion or numbness.
Understand: Learn how ADHD can affect executive functioning, emotional regulation, attention, memory, time, planning and follow-through.
Move Forward: Start building your own brain manual by naming what gets in the way and testing one support.

Your milestone: map three ADHD patterns you previously misunderstood.

The aim is not to fix yourself. The aim is to understand yourself with more accuracy and less shame.

Late diagnosis can bring a life review. Things that once looked like laziness, inconsistency, messiness, sensitivity or failure may start to look different.

This does not mean ADHD explains every part of your life. It means you may now have better language for patterns that were always there.

Start by noticing without rushing to judge. Ask yourself: What keeps coming back to my mind? What old labels am I questioning? What parts of my life now make more sense?

Workbook prompt

What are three things from your life that you are now seeing differently?

Diagnosis can bring relief, but it can also bring grief, anger, confusion or numbness.

You might feel validated one minute and furious the next. That does not mean you are doing it wrong. It means new information is landing in an old life.

You do not need to force the diagnosis into a positive story. You also do not need to make it tragic. Let it be honest.

Workbook prompt

What emotions come up when you realise these patterns may have had a reason?

ADHD can affect executive functioning, emotional regulation, attention, working memory, time awareness and everyday functioning.

That means some tasks may be harder than they appear from the outside. The point is not to excuse everything. The point is to understand what support might actually help.

When you can name the pattern, you can stop wasting energy on the wrong explanation.

Workbook prompt

Where do you notice executive function differences most clearly?

A brain manual is a practical record of what helps you function. It might include: what helps you start, what helps you remember, what drains you, what makes tasks harder, what helps you recover, what makes emotional regulation easier, and what support actually works.

This week, choose one pattern and test one support. Small is fine. Small is better than dramatic and abandoned by Tuesday.

Workbook prompt

What is one support you could try this week that works with your brain, not against it?

Week 1 Quick Reference

Download card

That Was ADHD Too?

Not everything is ADHD. But after late diagnosis, it is common to realise that some long-standing difficulties may have been connected to ADHD patterns.

  • Task initiation
  • Working memory
  • Time awareness
  • Emotional regulation
  • Impulse control
  • Organisation and planning
  • Following through
  • Switching tasks
  • Rest and recovery
  • Perceived criticism or rejection feeling intense
  • Sensory overload
  • Burnout from overcompensating

Use this card to notice patterns, not to shame yourself or diagnose others.
