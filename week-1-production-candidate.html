Scene 2 — Something Changed

What a late diagnosis actually does

It is not just new information

A late ADHD diagnosis is information arriving inside a life that was already built around explanations that were wrong, incomplete, or missing entirely.

It travels backwards

When that information arrives, it does not stay in the present. It moves across your life, into every place where something did not make sense, or where the explanation you were given was "not trying hard enough" or "too sensitive."

This disruption is recognised, not rare

The disruption that follows — to identity, to self-understanding, to the story you tell about yourself — is a documented part of what late diagnosis involves. You are not doing this wrong.

Scene 4 — How the Brain Manages Life

Your brain's management system

Executive function is the set of processes that help you start tasks, hold information, manage time, regulate emotion, and follow through. In ADHD, these work differently — not worse, differently.

"You looked capable because nobody could see the amount of effort required to stay that way."

Six areas, one system

Where you recognise something below, that's not a flaw on the map — it's a gap where support was missing. Naming the gap is the first step to filling it.
